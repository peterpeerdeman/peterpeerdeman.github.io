I've been tinkering with a cheap soldering iron before, but this was always keeping me from getting into hardware electronics projects. It was about time to get this skill sorted, so I dove into the youtube rabbit hole of soldering (thanks [nanofix](https://www.youtube.com/@nanofixca) and [joeydoestech](https://www.youtube.com/c/JoeyDoesTech)), bought some [soldering gear](https://notes.peterpeerdeman.nl/soldering-gear) and started practicing the basics that really help practical soldering: tip cleaning with brass, proper heat management of the iron and project, flux and leaded solder. I also picked up some soldering kits to experiment with, such as the [Velleman WSEDU08](https://www.velleman.eu/products/view/educational-lcd-oscilloscope-wsedu08/?id=460668), which helps you get some actual practice before you work on real world repairs.

![](../assets/images/2026-01-14-velleman-oscilloscope.jpg)

After the practice kit, I started churning through the repair projects that had been lining up the years before. Finally putting some headers on microcontroller development boards, wiring a display and battery for a LoRa board, replacing the clock capacitor in an original Xbox. The new soldering skill has also broadened my hardware modification skills, if you're interested definitely check out my [growatt wifi stick esphome modification](./growatt-wifi-stick-modification-to-make-uart-work-with-esp-home.mdx).
