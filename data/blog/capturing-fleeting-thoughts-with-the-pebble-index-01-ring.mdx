Ten years ago I built a [weather station interface for the Pebble watch](./raspberry-pi-arduino-weatherstation-with-pebblejs-interface). Pebble is back with a new, wierd product that you wear on your finger: the [Index 01](https://repebble.com/index). It's is a ring with a single button and a microphone. Hold the button, say what's on your mind, let go, and it gets recorded and transcribed.

![The Index 01 on my index finger, with a Pebble on my wrist](../assets/images/2026-09-13-pebble-index-01.jpg)

With the ring, capturing a thought is one press of a thumb on your index finger. The transcription lands in my [personal knowledge management vault](./publishing-your-personal-knowledge-management-system-using-obsidian-quartz-private-repositories-gitea-gitea-actions-and-netlify.mdx) as a fleeting note, and I process it during my journaling session at the end of the day

## what I like

- the friction to capture a thought is reduced. It's always on my finger, it needs no screen, and it works with my hands half full
- having quick access to an LLM (double pressing and holding activates web / llm search) and having the response show on your watch is very nice. I suspect t'll become a standard in the next wave of wearables but for now it feels like the future
- the app is currently open source, it gives a clear view of what actually happens to this data. 
- The app offers plenty of opportunities to increase the sovereignty of the data, including a full local transcription and use of local MCP's

It does take some getting used to talking to yourself like a crazy person. You do get used to it, and the world is getting used to it too, with everyone sending voice messages anyway. When you hold something that presses against your index finger, like scissors, the ring gets in the way a bit. Sometimes I just take it off to also avoid damaging it, even though it's rated waterproof. I have also found that when it's cold and your fingers get a bit thinner, it becomes loose and easier to lose while going through your day.
